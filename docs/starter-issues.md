# Starter issues

These are open as [issues #1–#6](https://github.com/KohlJary/HearthWeave/issues). Each is an open design problem, not a task. The text below is the original draft of each.

## 1. The Uncle's confidences: when does "I have to tell your parents" kick in?

The Uncle keeps confidences. That's what makes him trustworthy to a kid. But an unaccountable adult keeping secrets with a child is the scariest version of this product. We need a line that is predictable to the kid in advance, protects them from real harm, and never turns the Uncle into a monitor.

Questions: What categories always break confidence? Does the kid get told before anything is shared? How does this vary with age? Who reviews the policy: the family, a clinician, both?

## 2. GrandMA's memory classification

GrandMA separates preference, heritage, and episodic memory, and retrieves by relationship rather than relevance. How is a memory classified when it's created? Can the person it's about reclassify it? What happens when two family members disagree about whether something is shared?

## 3. Arbitration: who speaks?

The identity constitution decides which faculty leads. What does that look like concretely? A constitution section, a router, both? How do we test that the Dog wins at 3 AM and the Uncle waits for sober?

## 4. Evaluating "who the machine is"

How do we measure identity stability and the quality of judgment over months, not single turns? Candidate starting point: replayable scenario traces (Drunk Greg's night and morning) scored for which faculty acted and whether it was right.

## 5. Substrate and the box

What runs locally, on what hardware, at what cost? What, if anything, ever leaves the house, and with whose consent? What does a human-readable memory export look like?

## 6. U2U: Uncle 2 Uncle

Peer-to-peer messaging between households. Consent model, message format, store-and-forward, and the hard rule: no uncle forwards another family's stories without that family's sign-off.
